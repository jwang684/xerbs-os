# Diagnosis Pattern Definitions — Pattern Meanings

**Diagnosis Pattern Definitions · Content Sprint · Authoring (v1).** Status:
**PROPOSED — PENDING REVIEW.** This is authored knowledge content for the
`diagnosisPatternDefinitions` interface: the diagnosis-tier **meaning** layer. Each entry
pairs a **referenced canonical pattern identity** (owned by `diagnosisPatternCatalog`,
shown in bold) with an **objective definition** of that pattern's intrinsic clinical
meaning. It is not yet wired to the loader/registry/contract (a later step).

**Entry model (per the frozen Specification `docs/architecture/47`):** exactly two
conceptual fields — a referenced canonical pattern and its objective definition. Nothing
else.

**Definition boundary (Intrinsic Pattern Identity):** a definition states only *what the
pattern is* — its intrinsic pathological nature, the imbalance and organ/substance dynamics
it denotes. It never states when the pattern should be diagnosed, what treatment or formula
follows, or any symptom checklist, diagnostic procedure, recommendation, or mapping. It
names no treatment principles, formulas, or herbs.

Every referenced pattern exists in the frozen `diagnosis-pattern-catalog.md` (v1).

## Definitions

- **Excess Heat** — A diagnosis pattern in which an exuberant heat pathogen contends with intact upright qi, producing a state of excess heat within the body.
- **Excess Cold** — A diagnosis pattern in which an exuberant cold pathogen obstructs yang qi and congeals movement, producing a state of excess cold.
- **Deficiency Heat** — A diagnosis pattern in which insufficient yin fails to restrain yang, so that heat arises from within on a background of depletion.
- **Deficiency Cold** — A diagnosis pattern in which insufficient yang fails to warm the body, so that cold arises from within on a background of depletion.
- **Qi Deficiency** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by insufficient qi, with weakened powers of movement, warming, transformation, and holding.
- **Qi Stagnation** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by impaired free flow of qi, so that qi becomes constrained and unmoving in the affected region.
- **Qi Sinking** — A diagnosis pattern in which deficient qi loses its lifting function and descends, failing to hold organs and substances in their proper position.
- **Qi Counterflow** — A diagnosis pattern in which qi moves against its normal direction, ascending or rebelling instead of descending or coursing smoothly.
- **Blood Deficiency** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by insufficient blood failing to nourish and moisten the tissues, organs, and spirit.
- **Blood Stasis** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by impaired blood movement, with blood congealed or obstructed and no longer flowing freely.
- **Blood Heat** — A diagnosis pattern in which heat enters and agitates the blood, accelerating and disordering its movement.
- **Blood Cold** — A diagnosis pattern in which cold congeals the blood, slowing and obstructing its flow within the channels.
- **Qi and Blood Deficiency** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by simultaneous insufficiency of qi and blood, with combined failure of movement and nourishment.
- **Yin Deficiency** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by depleted yin, with diminished cooling, moistening, and restraint of yang.
- **Yang Deficiency** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by depleted yang, with diminished warming, activation, and transformation.
- **Yin Collapse** — A diagnosis pattern in which yin is suddenly and critically exhausted, an acute failure of the body's fluid and yin foundation.
- **Yang Collapse** — A diagnosis pattern in which yang is suddenly and critically exhausted, an acute failure of the body's warming and activating foundation.
- **Body Fluid Deficiency** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by depletion of body fluids, with loss of moistening throughout the body.
- **Dampness** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by accumulated dampness, a heavy, lingering, turbid pathogen that obstructs the movement of qi.
- **Damp-Heat** — A diagnosis pattern in which dampness and heat bind together into a sticky, lingering pathogenic combination.
- **Cold-Dampness** — A diagnosis pattern in which cold and dampness combine, congealing and obstructing yang qi and movement.
- **Phlegm-Dampness** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by accumulated phlegm and dampness, a thick turbid pathogen obstructing the movement of qi.
- **Phlegm-Heat** — A diagnosis pattern in which phlegm and heat combine into a hot, obstructive turbid pathogen.
- **Phlegm-Fluids** — A diagnosis pattern characterized by thin watery fluids accumulating and lodging where they do not belong.
- **Water Retention** — A diagnosis pattern in which the transformation and movement of water fails, so that water accumulates and overflows the tissues.
- **Wind-Cold Invasion** — A diagnosis pattern in which external wind and cold lodge in the exterior, obstructing the defensive qi.
- **Wind-Heat Invasion** — A diagnosis pattern in which external wind and heat lodge in the exterior, disturbing the defensive qi and the lung.
- **Wind-Dampness Obstruction** — A diagnosis pattern in which external wind and dampness lodge in the channels, sinews, and joints, obstructing their flow.
- **Summerheat Invasion** — A diagnosis pattern in which the seasonal summerheat pathogen invades, scattering qi and consuming fluids.
- **Dryness Invasion** — A diagnosis pattern in which external dryness invades, consuming fluids and impairing the moistening of the lung and exterior.
- **Wind-Water** — A diagnosis pattern in which external wind disrupts the lung's regulation of water, so that water accumulates acutely in the exterior.
- **Lesser Yang Pattern** — A diagnosis pattern in which a pathogen lodges at the pivot between exterior and interior, in the lesser yang, a half-exterior half-interior disharmony.
- **Heat Entering the Nutritive Level** — A diagnosis pattern in which a heat pathogen penetrates inward to the nutritive level, scorching the fluids and disturbing the spirit.
- **Heat Entering the Blood Level** — A diagnosis pattern in which a heat pathogen penetrates to the blood level, agitating the blood and stirring reckless movement.
- **Liver Wind** — A diagnosis pattern in which internal wind generated by the liver stirs within, producing abnormal internal movement.
